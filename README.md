# Learn Asking the Right Questions as a Data Analyst with Phoebe

Six 45-minute sessions on the first meeting of any data project: how a data analyst gets the
decision, the current way of working and the real state of the data out of a business person who
knows nothing about data, in words that teach them something while they answer. No code. One
invented pharmacy chain, one operations director with 24 hidden facts, and a discovery-meeting
bench that counts what each way of asking is worth.

**Live site:** https://phoebefu6.github.io/learn-asking-right-questions-as-da-with-phoebe/

| # | Session | Signature thing |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Why the gap exists, and the Six Doors | The framework, canonical for all four sibling courses; the teach-back line |
| 2 | Reading their data state in plain words | A five-rung ladder as interview questions; the data-state map drawn from her answers |
| 3 | The analyst's questions, and the bench | Five kinds of question; the simulator; your own meeting scored beside the presets |
| 4 | Teaching while you ask | One concept per meeting; twelve teach-back lines for a person who has never seen a table |
| 5 | From pains to three joint projects | Her answers as an opportunity tree; three candidates with a first step each |
| 6 | The mock meeting and the one-page brief | A full run on the bench, then the brief she can read, sent within 24 hours |

The bench in session 3 (`assets/asking-live.js` + `assets/asking-bank-da.js`) runs the same
45-minute meeting five ways on the same printed fact sheet. On this sheet the fast yes-or-no
interview scores 15 and records five beliefs that are wrong; a shuffle averages 28 and ranges
from 8 to 47; the Six Doors in order score 48; tuned for an analyst, 52. Every count is computed
from the bank file in the browser. The company and the person are constructed and say so.

Four sibling courses share the framework, the company and the engine, one per role:
data analyst (this one), data scientist, data engineer, AI developer. Each marks a different door
to open widest and carries its own question bank.

by Phoebe Fu
