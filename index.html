Learn Asking the Right Questions as a Data Analyst with Phoebe

An operations director asks for a sales dashboard. Six weeks later there is a correct dashboard, nobody opens it, and the six outlets that were running out of chronic medication are still running out, because "dashboard" was the only word she had for that and nobody in the meeting asked what would be different if it worked. Most of what goes wrong in data work goes wrong in the first meeting, quietly, with both sides sure they agreed. Six sessions on running that meeting as a data analyst: the six doors every discovery conversation has, the order to open them, the plain second sentence that lets a business person learn how data work thinks while she answers, and a bench that puts one invented operations director with 24 hidden facts in your browser and counts what each way of asking is worth.

The six sessions 🎯 · from "we need a dashboard" to a brief she can read

The running case is Harbourline, an invented chain of 38 pharmacy and clinic outlets across Singapore and Malaysia, and its operations director Adeline Tan, who has never heard the word pipeline. Every fact on her sheet is printed in the bench's bank file; the course claims the mechanism, never the numbers as facts about pharmacies.

Reading their data state in plain words

A five-rung ladder of questions that gets the real state of the data out of a person who has never seen a schema: typed on a Friday or recorded by a till, who owns the codes, who holds the only login. You draw the data-state map from her answers.

The analyst's questions, and the bench

Five kinds of question, and one operations director with 24 hidden facts run five ways in your browser. The fast yes-or-no interview scores 15 and records five things that are wrong; the Six Doors score 48; tuned for an analyst, 52. Then you take the chair.

★ the bench 💬 Session 4 · core

Teaching while you ask

The second sentence on every question, rewritten for a person who has never seen a table. The one concept per meeting worth explaining properly, twelve teach-back lines, and how to tell a lesson from a lecture.

one concept per meeting 🌱 Session 5 · core

From pains to three joint projects

Her answers become an opportunity tree, the tree becomes three candidate projects with a first step each, and a prioritisation you write down. Why the first project is a pilot in four outlets and not a rollout to 38.

three candidates, one first step 📄 Session 6 · capstone

The mock meeting and the one-page brief

A full 45-minute run on the bench with your own sheet, scored, then the one-page brief she can read: what you heard, what you will build first, how you will both know, sent within 24 hours. Final scorecard.
